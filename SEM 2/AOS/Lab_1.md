Central Processing Unit (CPU): Often called the "brain" of the computer, the CPU executes
  instructions from computer programs, performing basic arithmetic, logic, controlling, and
  input/output operations. Its primary role is to process data and carry out computations, making it
  crucial for the computer's overall speed and performance.

  Motherboard: This is the main printed circuit board (PCB) found in general-purpose computers and
  other expandable systems. It holds and allows communication between many of the crucial electronic
  components of a system, such as the CPU, memory, and connectors for other peripherals. It serves
  as the central backbone of the computer.

  Random Access Memory (RAM): RAM is a form of computer memory that can be read and changed in any
  order, typically used to store working data and machine code. It allows data items to be accessed
  in nearly the same amount of time irrespective of their physical location within the memory,
  enabling fast access to currently running programs and data.

  Hard Disk Drive (HDD): An HDD is an electromechanical data storage device that stores and
  retrieves digital data using magnetic storage and rapidly rotating platters coated with magnetic
  material. It is a non-volatile storage device, meaning it retains stored data even when powered
  off, and is typically used for long-term storage of operating systems, applications, and files.

  Solid State Drive (SSD): An SSD is a solid-state storage device that uses integrated circuit
  assemblies as memory to store data persistently. Unlike HDDs, SSDs have no moving parts, which
  results in faster data access, lower latency, reduced power consumption, and greater durability.
  They are increasingly used as primary storage for operating systems and frequently accessed
  applications.

  Power Supply Unit (PSU): The PSU converts alternating current (AC) electric power from a wall
  outlet to low-voltage direct current (DC) power for the internal components of a computer. It is
  responsible for supplying stable and consistent power to all parts of the computer, ensuring their
  proper operation.

  Graphics Processing Unit (GPU): A GPU is a specialized electronic circuit designed to rapidly
  manipulate and alter memory to accelerate the creation of images, frames, or animations for output
  to a display device. GPUs are widely used in embedded systems, mobile phones, personal computers,
  workstations, and game consoles, and are increasingly crucial for tasks like artificial
  intelligence and machine learning due to their parallel processing capabilities.

  Cooling Fan: Computer cooling fans are used to draw cooler air into the computer case from the
  outside, expel warmer air from inside, and move air across a heat sink to cool a particular
  component. They are essential for maintaining optimal operating temperatures and preventing
  overheating of sensitive components like the CPU and GPU.

  Heat Sink: A heat sink is a passive heat exchanger that transfers heat generated by an electronic
  or mechanical device to a fluid medium, often air or a liquid coolant. Its purpose is to dissipate
  heat away from critical components, working in conjunction with cooling fans to prevent thermal
  damage and ensure stable performance.

  Optical Drive (CD/DVD/Blu-ray): An optical drive is a type of computer disk drive that uses laser
  light or electromagnetic waves within or near the visible light spectrum as part of the process of
  reading or writing data to or from optical discs. These drives are used to read and write data
  from CDs, DVDs, and Blu-ray discs, though their use has declined with the rise of digital
  distribution and solid-state storage.

  Network Interface Card (NIC): A NIC, also known as a network adapter, is a hardware component that
  connects a computer to a computer network. It allows the computer to communicate with other
  devices on a local area network (LAN) or over the internet, enabling data transfer and network
  access.

  Memory Card Reader: A memory card reader is a device for accessing data on a memory card such as a
  CompactFlash (CF), Secure Digital (SD), or MultiMediaCard (MMC). It typically connects to a
  computer via USB and allows users to transfer photos, videos, and other files from their memory
  cards to their computer.

  Front Panel Audio Jacks: These are ports located on the front of a computer case that allow users
  to easily connect headphones, microphones, or other audio devices without having to reach the back
  of the computer. They provide convenient access for audio input and output.

  Expansion Slots: Expansion slots are sockets on the motherboard into which expansion cards (also
  known as adapter cards) can be inserted. These cards are used to add functionality to a computer
  system that is not provided by the motherboard, such as enhanced graphics, additional network
  ports, or specialized input/output capabilities.

  CPU Socket: The CPU socket is a mechanical component that provides mechanical and electrical
  connections between a microprocessor and a printed circuit board (PCB). It allows the CPU to be
  installed and replaced without soldering, providing a secure interface for the processor to
  communicate with the rest of the system.
