Wi-Fi Adapter: A Wi-Fi adapter is a device that allows a computer or device to connect to a wireless
  local area network (WLAN). It enables the device to communicate over Wi-Fi, providing wireless internet
  access and network connectivity without the need for physical Ethernet cables.

Bluetooth Adapter: A Bluetooth adapter is a device that enables a computer or other electronic device
  to communicate wirelessly with other Bluetooth-enabled devices. It facilitates short-range wireless
  communication for peripherals like keyboards, mice, headphones, and smartphones, creating a personal
  area network (PAN).

USB Flash Drive: A USB flash drive is a data storage device that includes flash memory with an
  integrated USB interface. It is typically removable and rewritable, much smaller than an optical disc,
  and commonly used for storage, data backup, and transfer of computer files between devices.

External Hard Drive: An external hard drive is a portable storage device that connects to a computer
  via a cable (usually USB). It functions like an internal hard drive but is housed in an external
  enclosure, providing additional storage capacity for backups, large files, or extending a computer's
  storage without internal modification.

External Optical Drive: An external optical drive is a peripheral device that can read and/or write to
  optical discs (CDs, DVDs, Blu-rays) and connects to a computer, typically via USB. It serves the same
  function as an internal optical drive but offers portability and can be used with devices that lack a
  built-in drive.

Graphics Card: Also known as a video card or GPU, a graphics card is an expansion card that generates a
  feed of output images to a display device (like a monitor). It contains its own processing unit (GPU)
  and memory, specifically designed to accelerate the rendering of images, videos, and 3D graphics,
  crucial for gaming, video editing, and graphic design.

Sound Card: A sound card is an internal expansion card that facilitates the input and output of audio
  signals to and from a computer under the control of computer programs. It provides audio capabilities,
  enabling sound playback through speakers or headphones, and sound recording through a microphone.

TV Tuner Card: A TV tuner card is a computer component that allows television signals to be received by
  a computer display. It enables users to watch and sometimes record television programs on their
  computer, often supporting various broadcast standards like analog TV, digital TV (DVB-T, ATSC), or
  even satellite TV.

RAID Controller Card: A RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks) controller card is a hardware
  device that manages hard disk drives or solid-state drives in a computer or storage system to achieve
  data redundancy, improved performance, or both. It handles the organization and striping of data across
  multiple drives, presenting them as a single logical unit to the operating system.

Network Switch: A network switch is a networking hardware device that connects devices on a computer
  network by using packet switching to receive, process, and forward data to the destination device. It
  operates at the data link layer of the OSI model and improves network efficiency by directing traffic
  only to the ports involved in the communication.

Router: A router is a networking device that forwards data packets between computer networks. Routers
  perform the traffic directing functions on the Internet, and in home networks, they typically connect
  the local network to the internet (via a modem), allowing multiple devices to share an internet
  connection and communicate with each other.

Modem: A modem (modulator-demodulator) is a network hardware device that modulates one or more carrier
  wave signals to encode digital information for transmission and demodulates signals to decode the
  transmitted information. It converts digital signals from your computer into analog signals that can
  travel over phone lines, cable lines, or fiber optic lines, and vice-versa, enabling internet
  connectivity.

Firewall: A firewall is a network security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing
  network traffic based on predetermined security rules. It establishes a barrier between a trusted
  internal network and untrusted external networks (like the internet), preventing unauthorized access
  and protecting against various cyber threats.

Interactive Whiteboard: An interactive whiteboard is a large interactive display board in the form of a
  whiteboard. It can either be a standalone touchscreen computer used independently to perform tasks and
  operations or a connectable apparatus used as a touchpad to control computers from one to the other. It
  enhances presentations, teaching, and collaboration by allowing users to interact with projected
  content directly.

Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS): A UPS is an electrical apparatus that provides emergency power to a
  load when the input power source or mains power fails. It typically employs a battery that will "kick
  in" automatically when it detects a loss of power from the primary source, protecting against power
  outages, surges, and sags, and allowing connected equipment to shut down gracefully.

Surge Protector: A surge protector (or surge suppressor) is an appliance or device designed to protect
  electrical devices from voltage spikes. It attempts to limit the voltage supplied to an electric device
  by either blocking or shorting to ground any unwanted voltages above a safe threshold, safeguarding
  electronics from damage caused by power surges.

Front Panel USB Ports: These are Universal Serial Bus (USB) ports located on the front of a computer
  case. They provide convenient and easily accessible connection points for USB devices such as flash
  drives, external hard drives, smartphones, and other peripherals, without needing to reach the back of
  the computer.
